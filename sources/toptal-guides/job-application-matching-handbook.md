## 1. Matching process overview

Toptal’s matching process connects network talent with clients. Two ways to start:

1. Respond to an **Availability Request** (Job Interest Request)
2. Submit a **Job Application** via the Talent Portal

**Flow:** Matcher searches talent → sends Job Interest Request OR talent applies → matcher vets → introduces selected candidates via **Talent Cards** → client interviews → trial period → engagement.

**Important:** If you have not heard back on an application within **3+ days**, assume other talent may have been sent to the client. Matchers may return to current candidates or seek new ones on subsequent searches.

---

## 2. Availability requests (Job Interest Requests)

Matchers contact talent pre-verified on: job skills, hours available, rate, time zone overlap.

**Location:** Talent Portal → My Jobs → Matcher Review tab.

### Response guidelines

- **Respond quickly** — ideally within **8 hours**. Toptal aims to send talent profiles to clients within **24 hours** of posting.
- **Add relevant details** — link to projects addressing similar challenges/industries.
- **Use the JD** to craft a **client-focused** response.
- **Address specific requirements** — depth in client industry, relevant skills, solving similar needs; share examples.
- **Explain value** you would add to the client.
- **Include links, statistics, results, numbers** from projects.
- **Decline with reason** when not a fit — helps matchers send better jobs later.
- **Keep availability up to date** in the talent portal (top right).
- **Update Job Preferences** when needed.
- **Tweak public profile** to reflect skills/experience for the job interest.

## 3. Job preferences

Set in Settings (left corner of Home page). Filters:

- Skills included/excluded
- Commitment type
- Work type (remote/onsite)
- Engagement length
- Client type
- Other criteria

More relevant Job Interest Requests when preferences are set.

## 4. Multiple opportunities

- Apply to jobs in the Talent Portal when they match skill set, expertise, availability, and time zone.
- Can accept Job Interest Requests **and** apply to multiple jobs at once.
- **Do not hold off** on other applications while waiting to hear back — matchers see when you’re in the running for multiple roles; applying to multiple opportunities increases offer likelihood.

## 5. Job search advanced filters

Filter by: commitment type, posted date, workday overlap, client budget, client type, length. Include/exclude skills or keywords.

## 7. Structure of a job application

### Company information

Assess fit by location, industry, company type, and size:

- **Enterprise / Fortune 500** — may work with several Toptalers
- **Startups** — handful of employees, early-stage
- **Mid-market** — venture-backed or established SMBs
- **Toptal team engagements** — managed project delivery with delivery manager, PM, technical lead; vs. working directly with client team as IC

### Job title and job details

Read carefully to determine (a) whether the job is a fit; (b) how to appeal through relevant skills and experiences.

### Job hiring status

| Status | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| No one is currently interviewing | Client accepting applications, not interviewing yet | **Excellent time to apply** |
| Candidates introduced to the client | Matcher sent potential matches | Apply ASAP if no Job Interest Request received |
| Client is interviewing candidates | Still can apply | New applications **less likely** to be considered |
| Not enough hours | Below minimum weekly hours | Cannot apply until hours updated |

### Commitment type

- Full-time (40 hrs/week)
- Part-time (20 hrs/week)
- Hourly (varies)

Extra hours beyond stated commitment: inform matcher; log as “extra hours” on platform.

### Job type

- **Remote** — worldwide except sanctioned regions
- **Location stated** — must have work permission and ability to relocate; discuss with client/matcher

### Working hours and overlap

- Be transparent about working hours; many clients are US-based.
- Some clients want similar time zones; others need fewer meetings.
- **Aim for at least two hours of overlap** with client working hours (minimum stated in posting).
- Update **Flexible Hours** in Settings → Scheduling if you can meet outside normal hours occasionally.

### Required and optional skills

- Review **Required Skills** — do you have skills to deliver high-quality results? Majority of required skills needed.
- **Optional skills** — if you have them, list in profile and connect to experience; use to sell candidacy.

## 8. Effective job applications (profile requirements)

Matchers search by skills. Profile must support matching.

### Skills overlap

- Skills list must be **comprehensive and relevant** to target jobs.
- **Connect each skill to a job or project** in profile — matchers use this to validate skills and pitch you to clients.

### Work experience

- Work experience must **match skills** in profile.
- Include where/how skills were used, problems solved, contribution to project goals.
- Same employer for several years: organize by **initiatives, projects, or roles**.

### Portfolio projects

Matchers and clients closely review portfolio for fit.

**Minimum portfolio projects by field:**

| Field | Minimum |
|-------|---------|
| **Developers** | **5** |
| Designers | 6 |
| Finance experts | 3 |
| Project managers | 3 |
| Product managers | 3 |

### High-quality photo

Professional photo increases hire chance.

### External profiles

Link **GitHub, LinkedIn, Behance, personal website** — clients like work descriptions and samples.

### Workday overlap

Flexibility increases eligibility — only claim what you can follow through on.

### Rate

Adjust rate per job in Application Builder. Lower rate can increase competitiveness. Assume rate **cannot change** after submission.

## 9. Job application questions

Matchers ask additional questions to:

- Confirm availability and time zone coverage
- Avoid surprises about planned time off during onboarding
- Speed up process with info clients will need

**Rules:**

- Answer in **third person**.
- If answer is already in the pitch, write **“added in the pitch.”**

**Good example (e-commerce / Tableau):**  
For the past five years, [Talent name] launched multiple features for a B2B SaaS email marketing platform… ~35 million users… Tableau and Segment to analyze user behavior…

**Bad example:** “I am a leader in this industry.” / “I was an Agile coach… huge success.” (first person, vague)

## 10. Job application pitches

**Primary authority for pitch generation in refine-cv.** Agents must apply this section (and §11–12) before writing any pitch.

### Goals

When applying for a job, you will be asked to write a pitch. Main goals:

1. Attract the attention of **matchers and clients**
2. Hook clients into reading your **whole profile**
3. **Increase your chances of being interviewed**

### Focus

**The biggest difference between a poor, good, or outstanding pitch is focus.**

A **client-focused** pitch should address the **specific requirements of the job**. Crafting a successful pitch depends on understanding the client’s needs.

- The job posting gives key information — **also research the company and its culture** when possible.
- The more information you have on the company, project, or job, the better the pitch.
- Detail depth of experience in the client’s **industry**, in the **relevant skill**, and in **solving similar needs**.
- This personal story, including **specific examples and details**, should show you have the experience and tools needed.
- Explain **how you would add value** — use **links, statistics, results, and numbers** from your projects when possible.
- Mention **enthusiasm** regarding these types of projects — signals you’re invested in the project’s success.

### “Wow factor” pitch (The Pitch)

An outstanding pitch:

- **Balances** general career overview with impressive details
- **Stands out from the crowd** — shows personality and tells a story in **500 characters or less**
- Uses **specific examples** of what you’ve accomplished for clients, including **quantifiable results**
- Is **client focused:** “This is the value I can bring to you. This is why you want me on your team.”

### Questions to answer in a strong pitch

Weave verified answers into the pitch where they fit this JD (do not dump every bullet — **focus** on what the client needs):

| Theme | Questions |
|-------|-----------|
| **Where** | Where have you worked? What industries, companies, or types of companies? What size companies (e.g., annual revenue)? |
| **What you built** | What did you create (single or in aggregate)? How big was it (project budget/value, number of active users)? For what company or type of company — name well-known brands if you can. What were the results? |
| **Recognition** | What awards have you received? |
| **Scope of work** | What kind of work have you done? How many years of experience do you have? |
| **Expertise** | What are you most expert in (types of projects, methods, technologies)? What makes you stand out? What do you do better than others in your area(s) of focus? |
| **Identity** | Who are you professionally? What’s your specialty? What’s your most notable professional achievement? |

**Industry experience** helps even if past roles differ from your current focus — you can **lead with it if it’s really impressive**.

## 11. Strong application examples (patterns)

Use these patterns when writing pitches and application answers.

### Python Engineer (strong)

**What makes it strong:**

- Shows **why this project specifically** would be a good fit
- **Highlights and ranks skills clearly** (Python primary; JavaScript experience but “definitely not an expert” — honest qualification)
- Links to latest profile project with stack details (e.g., Starlette, GraphQL, PostgreSQL on AWS)
- Shows **passion for the work** (robotics competition, consulting on remote robotic systems)
- Addresses **availability** concretely: part-time→full-time transition, overlap hours (e.g., mornings EST), **interview windows** (days/times)

### Salesforce Consultant (strong)

**What makes it strong:**

- **Well written:** third person, free of grammatical errors
- Highlights **relevant skills** tied to the role (implementations, Apex, LWC, integrations)
- **Architect-level scope** with named responsibility (global Salesforce/CPQ architect)
- **Specific recent project** (SOAP APIs for real-time surveys, inbound email handlers)

---

## 12. Weak application examples (anti-patterns)

**Do not** produce pitches matching these patterns.

### iOS Developer (weak)

**What makes it weak:**

- Opens with “Hi, Sir!” — overly casual
- **First person** throughout (“I am…”, “my skill-set”, “I will really do my best”)
- **Vague and far too broad** — “so many apps”, “full experiences” without specifics
- **Repetitive**, poor grammar
- **Unnecessary last paragraph** promising to “win against anyone else”

### Brand and Web Designer — medical marketplace (weak)

**What makes it weak:**

- **Experience too wide** to be relevant to the position (Pardot, Sales Cloud, Social Studio unrelated to brand/web design JD)
- **Unclear which customer** talent is referring to
- **Numerous spelling and grammatical errors** (“marketting”, “pardot marketting”)

## 13. After you apply

- Hear back within **1–3 days** only if sent to client / invited to interview.
- Experiment with profile tweaks (availability, hours, rate) if struggling.
- Matchers **cannot** provide feedback on every application.
- Jobs get canceled — keep applying to strong fits even when in the running elsewhere.
